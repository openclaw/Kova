# Comparison: sessions

Product revision: 7e80556f503d26ddde64e274bfb50cbbec7830f5
Date of the recorded runs: 2026-10-13

This comparison preserves the recorded results from two historical rounds. The
procedure changed between rounds and after reconciliation. Neither round
recorded a heading-by-heading walk, so the current condition-heading shares
cannot be recovered from those records. Conversion to the current template
form is not a new derivation or evidence that the current acceptance rule was
met.

## Round 1

Union: not recorded for entry points or condition headings; no reconciled
individual-condition total was recorded.

| Run | Procedure revision | Entry points found | Condition headings found | Individual conditions found |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Before the round-1 corrections | 194 found; union and share not recorded | Not recorded | 42 |
| B | Before the round-1 corrections | 279 found; union and share not recorded | Not recorded | 46 |

The recorded placed counts were 166 of 194 for A and 222 of 279 for B. A
listed 24 operations and 35 phases; B listed 29 operations and 41 phases.
The recorded break mappings were 20 of 21 and 18 of 20 respectively. The
comparison described 23 common operations and about 32 common conditions;
that approximation is not a reconciled union or a share.

Remaining differences: A included the Home companion and excluded cloud-worker
moves; B made the opposite choices. Channel messaging, scheduled jobs, GitHub
publication and export were grouped differently. A read 20 documents and B
read 18, with 30 in their combined list. Consequently they found different
conditions, including goal budget, hidden browser tabs and model reversion
in A, and large stores, pinned-root protection, near-limit context, invalid
store selectors and loading catalogs in B. The subsequent procedure fixed the
area's bounds and document list before either run, and added setup phases,
phase paths, time bounds, and placeholder and trait conventions.

Acceptance: known shortfall; neither required share is available.
Rerun cap: the historical numeric cap was not recorded. This conversion uses
only the two recorded rounds and performs no additional runs.
Known shortfall: missing reconciled shares and heading records, compounded by
different bounds and document lists. The records do not establish nine-tenths
coverage for either run.

## Round 2

Union: 455 entry points, an unrecorded number of condition headings,
51 individual conditions. Of the entry points, 348 were placed in 31
operations and 107 were excluded.

| Run | Procedure revision | Entry points found | Condition headings found | Individual conditions found |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C | After the round-1 corrections: fixed area input, setup phases, phase paths and 300-second bound | 396/455 = 87.0% | Not recorded | 44; historical condition share 44/51 = 86.3% |
| D | After the round-1 corrections: fixed area input, setup phases, phase paths and 300-second bound | 393/455 = 86.4% | Not recorded | 35 in the run comparison; 34/51 = 66.7% after the recorded reconciliation's counting |

The recorded placed-entry shares were C 310/348 = 89.1% and D 315/348 =
90.5%. These are preserved for provenance; the current rule counts found
entry points, including exclusions. The original round comparison listed C
189 of 214 placed and D 136 of 159 placed. The later reconciliation cited
214 and 160 as the runs' original totals. That one-entry discrepancy in D
is retained as a historical counting difference, without inventing a revised
count. Similarly, D's 35 original conditions and 34 normalized conditions are
distinct recorded figures.

Remaining differences: Both runs kept the Home companion and excluded
cloud-worker moves. C grouped several operations more broadly, while D split
search, archive and restore, repair, and thread-bound subagent work. C added
commenting on selected reply text. D added two linked subagent documents.

C missed 59 reconciled entry points, primarily individual New session,
composer, Subagents and file-panel controls; recovery and startup paths; the
HTTP history endpoint; and exclusions for plugin hooks, swarm calls, Gateway
service commands, device rotation and memory search. D missed 62, primarily
shared chat commands and consumed New session dependencies; link routes;
selection comments; team-mode and inspection controls; and calls from other
areas. Enumeration granularity and exclusion rules explain these differences.

The historical condition comparison recorded seven missing conditions in C:
DM isolation with identity links, configured automatic reset, Home's creation
notice, search reconciliation after import or rewind, manual compaction,
reset clearing goal and card, and concurrent database reads and writes. It
recorded 17 missing in D: rolling main history, nested children and forks,
disk budget, public links, imported drafts, active goals, saved progress cards,
multiple agents with a global key, synthetic automation rows, stale peer-DM
rows, stale dashboards, paired CLI profiles, heartbeat, fresh cron sessions,
RTL locale, settings reload and Gateway switching during deletion. The older
procedure did not consistently distinguish lasting persona state, automatic
events and manual operations. Individual-condition shares therefore cannot
substitute for the current condition-heading shares.

Acceptance: known shortfall. Both found-entry shares are below nine tenths,
and neither condition-heading share is available.
Rerun cap: the historical numeric cap was not recorded. This conversion uses
only the two recorded rounds and performs no additional runs.
Known shortfall: C's entry-point share is 87.0% and D's is 86.4%; missing
heading records prevent measuring the other required share. Historical counts
also include classifications the current procedure treats differently,
including broadcast events as entry points and manual actions as conditions.
The converted template represents all 60 recorded phases in 68 current phases
after dividing the three setup sequences into seven bounded setup phases and
separating three persona-read groups from runtime-result consumers, plus an
explicit RPC target setup. The phase
order follows the planner dependencies, and the duplicate deletion-time search
is covered by the identical search-history command. The template labels these limits; it does not claim a fresh source enumeration or current-rule acceptance.


The current reset and recovery records explicitly prepare their target state,
and creation-capable command records declare generated identities. The added
recovery preparation helper is unfinished, so that phase remains withheld.
These contract repairs are not another derivation round or live execution proof.
